Rill Decision Lab

Rill is a 34M-parameter flow-matching decision model. Give it a piece of text (the state) and a few typed questions: yes/no, one-of-many choice, or an ordered score. It returns a probability for every option from one forward pass, and a small calibrator trained from outcome-only rewards sets how sure it should be. The model runs in your browser (WebAssembly, or plain JavaScript where that is unavailable), so nothing you type leaves this device.

The probabilities come from a single pass at flow time t = 0, which the flow-matching objective makes a proper estimate of the answer distribution. τ is the temperature that the RL-trained calibrator picks for each question: above 1 it softens an overconfident answer, and it never changes which option wins.

Examples are illustrative, not real customer data, and use task types the model was trained on (banking intents, reviews, news topics). On unfamiliar tasks it is less accurate, but in our tests it was rarely confidently wrong (0.5% of questions from unseen sources at confidence ≥ 0.9). Model: Rill-32M (Ettin encoder with DiT flow blocks, English-only) with its outcome-reward calibrator, int8 embeddings and 8-bit weights. The System-1 readout is one pass at flow time t = 0. Probabilities are conditional on the options you supply, and confidence means the probability of the top option. This is a research model: verify consequential decisions.
